FlashCards

Catch a word from whatever you are reading, study it a few days later, with the sentence you actually met it in.

The problem

People who study or work in a second language struggle to find a seamless way to learn vocabulary. You are reading a case or a paper for class, you hit a word you do not know, and going off to a translator takes enough effort that you either skip it or look it up and forget it by the end of the paragraph. It does not stick.

So I built one that gets out of the way. You save a word by clicking one button on your bookmarks bar, and it builds the card for you: what the word means as a verb and what it means as a noun, an example sentence, and a recording of someone actually saying it.

What I built

Two halves: catching and studying. A bookmarklet sits on the favourites bar. Select a word on any page, click it, and the word is saved with the sentence around it.

The meaning arrives on its own. Wiktionary for the definitions and an example, Simple Wiktionary for a plainer version of the same thing, with the full dictionary one button away. Wikipedia as a last resort, which is how a word like evals gets an answer at all.

Four separate spaces. Currently works well in English, Spanish, German and French. They do not share a pile: each keeps its own words, its own decks and its own sitting, and switching between them is one click.

Three answers, not two. Not yet, Almost, Knew it. A card you did not get stays due today. Almost brings it back in two days, and Knew it sends it sixteen days out.

German and French nouns carry their article. The card says das Haus, not Haus, and une école, not école. A noun without its gender is a noun learned wrong, and the gender is not guessable from the word. French takes un and une rather than le and la, because those two both shrink to l’ in front of a vowel and hide the thing the article is there to show.

It works on the phone, and offline. Added to the home screen it opens like an app, with no browser chrome and no address bar. Sign in with Google and the words follow you between the laptop and the phone; stay signed out and they never leave the browser you saved them in.

Stack

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework and no build step. Cloudflare Workers and D1 for the sign-in and the sync. Wiktionary, Simple Wiktionary, Wikipedia, Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons for everything on the card. Written with Claude Code.

Open FlashCards — it starts empty, and signing in is optional.
