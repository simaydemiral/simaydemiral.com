Electrical noise measurement points to pre-formed electron pairs

A team led by Milan Allan of Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich and Ingmar Swart of Utrecht University used a scanning tunneling microscope to measure shot noise in a cuprate, a method that can show whether current is carried by single electrons or Cooper pairs. The Science study addresses the long-debated "pseudogap" state, where electrons appear to go missing above the superconducting temperature; in cuprates the pairing signal is weaker than in conventional superconductors because single electrons keep flowing alongside pairs.

KEY   cuprates found to superconduct at higher temperatures in the late 1980s · unexplained for nearly 40 years · conventional superconductors show a clean doubling of noise · about four years of student work to make the instrument sensitive enough

Foreign investors net sellers of Turkish securities

Foreign investors sold a net $3.65 billion in Turkish stocks and bonds over seven weeks through October 30, mostly in bonds, according to a report by the financial news website Ekonomim. Council of Higher Education President Erol Özvar said Saturday that Turkey is preparing to cut undergraduate degrees from four years to three, completed with 180 credits on European models, arguing that long study delays young people's entry into working life at a time of falling fertility and later marriage.
