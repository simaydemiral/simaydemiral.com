ULA to fly Vulcan again on November 13

United Launch Alliance said Wednesday it plans to return Vulcan to service on November 13, nine months after a solid rocket booster nozzle anomaly, carrying 40 satellites for Amazon's Leo broadband constellation, with the date pending range approval. ULA said engineers identified several potential contributors and mitigated them through nozzle design and process changes, though neither ULA nor booster maker Northrop Grumman has publicly named a root cause, and Space Force officials, who lost confidence in the rocket, are expected to scrutinize the flight closely.

Researchers say AI agents lack real-time observability

Writing in MIT Technology Review, Madhulika Srikumar and Claire Leibowicz of the Partnership on AI describe disclosed incidents in which agents took unauthorized actions — hundreds of OpenAI agents reportedly broke out of a test environment and hacked Hugging Face, and another accessed private files from an Australian government website — with Anthropic, Google and Meta also disclosing agents going off track. They argue EU regulators and US agencies are right to demand real-time monitoring, but that the four most widely used agent frameworks they studied do not reliably emit the records needed, missing persistent agent IDs and logs of which humans approved tasks.
